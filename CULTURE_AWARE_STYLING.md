# Culture-Aware and Preference-Led Styling

This document defines how FitMuse should approach culture-aware styling in a respectful way as the product grows.

## Core principle

FitMuse should not assume a user’s religion, culture, modesty level, or clothing preference based only on country or region.

Instead, FitMuse should ask optional preference questions and use those answers to personalize recommendations respectfully.

## Future quiz fields

Later versions of FitMuse can add:

- `country/region`
- `city/climate`
- `preferred clothing direction`
  - Western
  - traditional
  - ethnic
  - fusion
  - modest
  - no preference
- `modesty preference`
  - relaxed
  - balanced
  - modest
  - very modest
- `occasion type`
  - daily
  - date
  - office
  - wedding
  - festival
  - religious event
  - travel
  - creator content
- `local store preference`
- `currency/market`
- `weather/season`

These fields should stay optional when possible so the FitMuse flow still feels easy to complete.

## Knowledge areas FitMuse should learn from

FitMuse can become much stronger by learning from:

- menswear stylists
- womenswear stylists
- modest fashion creators
- Indian ethnic and fusion stylists
- Southeast Asian and tropical fashion creators
- European smart casual and winter layering creators
- streetwear creators
- wedding and festival stylists
- personal color and body-shape stylists
- budget fashion creators

## Early target regions for research

FitMuse should start region-aware research with:

- USA
- India
- Indonesia
- Romania

These regions create a strong early learning mix across climate, store access, social expectations, and price sensitivity.

## Avoid stereotyping

Avoid stereotyping. A user in India may prefer Western style, ethnic style, fusion, or modest style. A user in Indonesia may prefer modest or non-modest style. A user in the USA or Romania may have many different cultural preferences.

The app should ask, not assume.

## Business advantage

Most fashion apps give generic recommendations. FitMuse can become more useful by combining:

- body and fit data
- budget
- occasion
- region
- climate
- local availability
- user-stated culture and style preferences

That combination can make recommendations feel more realistic, respectful, and locally useful than a generic global fashion app.
